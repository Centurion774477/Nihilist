Nihilism is more or less a philosophical view (formally it is a family of views) that rejects any sort of objective truths
 such as purpose, knowledge, and ethics.

For some nihilists — namely epistemological nihilists — this rejection comes from the belief that nothing can be truly
known, which is why a true and absolute nihilist would believe in nothing; they would have no loyalties, morals, or 
boundaries.

Nihilism challenges the things the world has accepted as normal — they ask questions like: “Why are bad things 
bad?”. With that, they blatantly reject any universal truths, with a key one being any sort of religion. Religion is
basically the antithesis of Nihilism — most religions involve morals, purpose, and ethics, all of which are rejected in 
Nihilism.

Nihilists have had quite a destructive history, being first truly seen in a loosely organized Russian revolutionary movement circa 1860-1880s,
where they would reject authorities like the state and church. These nihilists loudly denounced God and any other form of religion, 
claiming they are harmful to freedom.

However, the roots of Nihilism can be traced as far as to the Skeptics, who would denounce traditional truths as unjustifiable opinions.
Later nihilism would take these beliefs a bit further, dogmatically denying truths,
compared to the Skeptics who would do so in a more sensible and curious manner.

There are many different forms of nihilism: political, rejecting any and all political, social, and religious order; ethical or moral,
rejecting absolute and dogmatic moral or ethical values; epistemological, rejecting the possibility of truth; and existential nihilism,
which states life has no obvious meaning or value.

I think Nihilism is a harmful belief on its own, especially when practiced in extreme forms, but taking ideas from it,
such as not being afraid to question the truth, can be beneficial to your personal beliefs.

I myself am not a nihilist, as I believe in morals, religion, and loyalties, but my OCaml code, titled “Nihilist”, is (to some extent) — 
it exists to reject the boundaries and rules laid out by KrabbaScript, to allow us to write code according to our personal rules and convictions,
instead of conforming to style dogma.
